# Independent proof and trust-boundary review

Status: **PASS for the stated finite phase-free binary-symplectic theorems.** The independent source-only rebuild, final source identity checks, and transitive axiom audit all passed. The scope boundaries below are part of this verdict.

## What the theorem must mean

The formal objects are finite **phase-free binary-symplectic Paulis**: two `BitVec n` values, addition by XOR, and support weight equal to the number of sites where either bit is set. `InSpan` is the closure of the supplied rows under zero and addition. `Normalizes` means symplectic orthogonality to every supplied row. `Logical` means normalizing and outside that full span. `HasDistance S 4` must supply both a universal lower bound over every Pauli and a nontrivial weight-four witness.

The proposed `HasBinaryDimension` is a substantive basis definition. It requires a spanning family of the stated size, equality of its closure-defined span with the supplied row span, and independence against every Boolean coefficient vector. The pivot recovery theorem and two directions of change-of-generator certificates are a valid way of proving it. Merely returning a Gaussian-elimination integer would not suffice.

The distance certificate is likewise a substantive bridge. Exact singleton syndrome columns are bound to actual supplied rows. A predicate includes zero and all singleton syndromes, excludes sums of errors at two distinct sites, and singleton columns are nonzero. The generic theorem uses the actual nodup support of an arbitrary Pauli and derives that a normalizer of weight below four has empty support. It is not restricted to the enumerated input errors. The L=11 optimization checks ordered distinct-site pairs; the formal bridge recovers the opposite order using XOR commutativity with both sites and both axes swapped. Site equality is excluded by the actual nodup support, so no diagonal case is lost.

## Phases and physical interpretation

The JSON stores no generator signs. A symplectic theorem alone must not be described as a formal Hilbert-space codespace theorem.

For these particular inputs, an independent signed-Pauli calculation confirms that the positive Hermitian representatives are consistent. The binary relation space has dimension three, generated by:

1. the sum of all X000 and all X111 checks;
2. the sum of all Z100 and all Z011 checks;
3. the sum of all X000, all Z100, and all Y010 checks.

Each of the eight relations multiplies to +I. The third relation reduces to global X, global Z, and global Y; the number of qubits is a multiple of eight. These phase calculations are recorded in `independent_candidate_audit.json` and remain external to the finite-code theorem.

A careless inference from commutation to an all-positive stabilizer would be false: the pairwise commuting operators XX, ZZ, YY have product −I and no simultaneous +1 eigenspace. `AuditSanity.lean` kernel-checks the corresponding signed-bit product counterexample. Other semantic regression tests show that a product can be in the stabilizer span without being a supplied generator, duplicated nonzero rows are dependent, and Y contributes one to Pauli support weight. The six tests compiled with only `propext` reported by `#print axioms`.

The standard passage from a suitable signed stabilizer of binary rank r to a complex codespace of dimension 2^(n-r), and its quantum error-correction interpretation, must be identified as external unless a separate Hilbert-space formalization is added. No intrinsic non-CSS theorem, infinite-family theorem, asymptotic distance, or literature-novelty result is implied by these two finite binary certificates.

## Kernel trust requirements

The accepted proof must use no `sorry`, `admit`, custom axioms, `native_decide`, `Lean.ofReduceBool`, native-backed `bv_decide`, or kernel-check bypasses. Plain `decide`, `rfl`, simplification, and ordinary proof-producing tactics are appropriate, subject to final axiom inspection. Standard logical axioms such as `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` are distinct from added empirical assumptions.

Official Lean documentation explains that elaborated proof terms are checked by its kernel, that `#print axioms` detects transitive assumptions, and that native evaluation broadens the trust boundary. For Lean 4.19, native evaluation is exposed through `Lean.trustCompiler`. A clean source build, source review, and transitive axiom checks were completed; this is not an independently implemented kernel/comparator certification.
